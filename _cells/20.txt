## 4. Persistent Checkpointing

### 4.1 Why Checkpointing Matters for EDA

In analog EDA, a single post-layout simulation with parasitic extraction can run for **48+ hours**. If the machine fails mid-run, without checkpointing the entire multi-iteration design loop must restart from scratch.

LangGraph provides two built-in checkpointers:

| Checkpointer | Use Case | Persistence |
|---|---|---|
| `MemorySaver` | Development / testing | In-memory only (lost on restart) |
| `SqliteSaver` | Production | SQLite database on disk |

The checkpointer records:
1. The **full state** at each node transition.
2. The **thread ID** (allowing multiple concurrent design runs).
3. A **checkpoint ID** for each snapshot (enabling time-travel).

### 4.2 Formal Checkpoint Model

A checkpoint $c_k$ at step $k$ is:

$$
c_k = \bigl(\text{thread\_id}, \; k, \; s_k, \; n_{\text{next}}, \; t_k\bigr)
$$

where $s_k$ is the state, $n_{\text{next}}$ is the next node to execute, and $t_k$ is the timestamp.
