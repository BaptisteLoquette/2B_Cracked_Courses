## 6. Time-Travel Debugging

### 6.1 Motivation

When a design loop converges (or fails) after many iterations, the engineer needs to understand **why** specific decisions were made. Traditional logging gives a flat trace; LangGraph's time-travel lets you:

1. **Inspect** the full state at any historical node execution.
2. **Replay** the graph from any checkpoint with modified state.
3. **Compare** states across iterations to identify the parameter changes that drove convergence.

This is analogous to `git bisect` for hardware design: find the exact iteration where a constraint was first satisfied (or violated).

### 6.2 Formal Model

Given a sequence of checkpoints $\{c_0, c_1, \ldots, c_K\}$, time-travel allows:

$$
\text{replay}(G, c_k, \Delta s) = G.\text{invoke}(s_k \;\|\; \Delta s)
$$

where $\Delta s$ is an optional state override injected at replay time.
